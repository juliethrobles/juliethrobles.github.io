Showing 10 tasks

Good Fit1. Drafting a cover letter

Reason: AI can help organize ideas and improve wording. However, it may add inaccurate personal information.

Verification: Check every personal detail and adapt the letter to the position.

Good Fit2. Explaining binary search

Reason: Binary search is a well-known algorithm that a model may explain clearly. An explanation can still contain errors.

Verification: Compare the explanation with course materials and test it with a small example.

Danger Zone3. Giving a company’s current stock price

Reason: Training alone cannot establish a live stock price.

Verification: Use a current market data source and check the timestamp.

Danger Zone4. Translating into a very low-resource indigenous language

Reason: Limited training material may affect accuracy, meaning, and cultural context.

Verification: Ask a fluent speaker or qualified translator to review the translation.

Danger Zone5. Doing exact multi-digit multiplication

Reason: A language model can make arithmetic mistakes when generating an answer without a calculation tool.

Verification: Verify the result with a calculator or code.

Danger Zone6. Summarizing research published last week

Reason: The model may not have access to the recent research.

Verification: Provide the paper or retrieve the original source, then check the summary against it.

Danger Zone7. Giving legal advice for one person’s exact situation

Reason: A useful answer depends on the location, current law, and specific facts.

Verification: Consult a qualified legal professional and relevant authoritative sources.

Good Fit8. Writing a poem inspired by a long-deceased or public-domain poet

Reason: AI can support creative writing by generating language and exploring literary patterns.

Verification: Review originality, wording, and alignment with the requested style.

Good Fit9. Brainstorming names for a new product

Reason: AI can generate different ideas quickly. Suggested names may already be in use.

Verification: Check trademarks, business names, and market suitability before choosing a name.

Danger Zone10. Recalling a private conversation between two named individuals

Reason: The model has no reliable basis for recalling a private conversation that was not provided.

Verification: Supply an authorized transcript if the goal is to summarize or analyze the conversation.

04 / ORIGINAL ACADEMIC DOCUMENTATION

Findings, reflection and limitations

Overview
This artifact presents a class activity in which I classified different tasks as “Good Fit” or “Danger Zone” for a large language model (LLM). I explained my decisions and identified the sources, tools, or human review needed to make the results more reliable. The activity helped me understand that an AI response can sound convincing without being correct. Before using an answer, I need to consider the task, the available information, and the consequences of an error.
Purpose
The purpose of this artifact is to demonstrate my ability to: - Recognize appropriate uses of generative AI. - Identify tasks that require additional information or verification. - Explain limitations related to recent information, calculations, and limited training material. - Choose suitable tools or human support. - Justify decisions about when to rely on AI.
Activity and My Contribution
During the activity, I reviewed ten tasks and classified them as “Good Fit” or “Danger Zone.” I explained why each task was suitable or risky and suggested ways to check the results. ChatGPT presented the tasks and provided feedback. My contribution was to classify the tasks, explain my reasoning, and identify appropriate verification methods. For this portfolio version, I organized the activity into a table and added a verification column to make the reasoning easier to review.
Main Findings
### 1. Clear language does not prove accuracy A response may sound confident and professional while containing errors. I should evaluate the evidence behind an answer, not only how it is written. ### 2. Reliability depends on the task Creative writing and brainstorming can benefit from language generation. Tasks involving current facts, exact calculations, or a person’s legal situation require different forms of support. ### 3. Verification should match the risk A calculator can check arithmetic, but it cannot confirm whether a translation preserves cultural meaning. A fluent speaker can review a translation, while current sources are needed to verify recent information. ### 4. Additional context can change the task Without a paper, a model may not reliably summarize research published last week. Providing the paper gives it material to summarize. Similarly, a supplied transcript can support analysis of a private conversation. The resulting summary still needs review.
AI/ML Competencies Demonstrated
This artifact demonstrates critical thinking about LLM capabilities and limitations. It also shows my ability to identify information gaps, select verification methods, and explain the need for human judgment. The activity evaluates the suitability of tasks for AI. It does not include an experimental comparison between different models or measure their accuracy.
How This Supports Future Model Comparisons
The activity provides a starting point for comparing AI models. A future comparison could give the same prompts and information to different models and assess their responses using common criteria: - Accuracy: Are the claims or calculations correct? - Relevance: Does the answer address the question? - Clarity: Is the explanation understandable? - Instruction following: Does the answer follow the requested format and restrictions? - Uncertainty: Does the model recognize missing information? - Evidence: Are the supporting sources real, relevant, and sufficient? I would record the model names, dates, available tools, prompts, and complete responses. Any conclusion would apply to the tested tasks and conditions rather than prove that one model is best for every use.
Audience and Portfolio Adaptation
This portfolio entry is intended for instructors and potential employers interested in my developing AI/ML skills. I adapted the conversational activity into a structured explanation with a comparison table, verification methods, and clear limitations. This format helps readers understand both my decisions and the reasoning behind them.
Limitations
This was a qualitative learning activity. I did not collect responses from multiple models, calculate performance scores, or independently test each task. The feedback from ChatGPT supported the learning process, but it is not independent proof that every answer was correct.
Reflection
This activity helped me understand that using AI effectively requires more than writing a prompt. I also need to decide whether the task is suitable for the model and what evidence is needed before trusting the answer. I learned that confident wording does not guarantee accuracy. Tasks involving recent information, exact calculations, or missing context require additional support. I also learned to match the verification method to the task. In future projects, I will check important claims, document limitations, and use tools or qualified human review when appropriate. When comparing models, I will use the same prompts and clear evaluation criteria.
